# Methodology

1-2 sentence intro: "This page explains how the calculator derives carrier frequencies from allele frequencies and computes recurrence risks. All calculations follow Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium principles."

## Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium

Brief plain-language explanation: In a large, randomly mating population, allele and genotype frequencies remain constant across generations. This principle lets us predict how common carrier status is from the frequency of the disease-causing allele.

Hardy-Weinberg genotype frequency table (use Unicode math):

| Genotype | Frequency | Meaning |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| AA (homozygous normal) | p² | Unaffected, no pathogenic variants |
| Aa (heterozygous carrier) | 2pq | Carries one pathogenic variant |
| aa (homozygous affected) | q² | Affected with two pathogenic variants |

Where **q** = disease allele frequency, **p** = 1 − q, and p + q = 1.

## Calculating Carrier Frequency

### Allele Frequency Aggregation

Explain:
- The calculator sums allele frequencies across all qualifying pathogenic variants in a gene
- For each variant: `variant_AF = (exome_AC + genome_AC) / (exome_AN + genome_AN)`
- Combined disease allele frequency: `q = sum of all variant AFs`
- This aggregation handles genes with many pathogenic variants (like CFTR) where each individual variant is rare but the combined carrier frequency is clinically significant

### From Allele Frequency to Carrier Frequency

Explain:
- Carrier frequency = 2pq (heterozygous genotype frequency)
- When q is small (as is typical for rare disease alleles): 2pq ≈ 2q
- The calculator uses the approximation: **carrier_frequency = 2 × q**
- This approximation is accurate when q < 0.05 (5%), which covers virtually all autosomal recessive conditions

::: info Why the Approximation Works
For a gene with combined allele frequency q = 0.02 (2%): exact 2pq = 2 × 0.98 × 0.02 = 0.0392. Approximation 2q = 0.04. The difference is < 0.001 (0.1%). For most clinical purposes, this is negligible.
:::

### Population-Specific Calculations

Explain:
- The calculator performs separate calculations for each gnomAD population
- Each population has its own allele count (AC) and allele number (AN), yielding population-specific AFs
- This is important because carrier frequencies can vary substantially between populations due to founder effects
- A founder effect is flagged when a population's frequency exceeds 5x the global frequency (configurable threshold)

## Recurrence Risk

Explain that recurrence risk depends on the index patient's status:

### Heterozygous Carrier (risk = carrier_frequency / 4)

Scenario: The index patient is a known carrier. The recurrence risk for offspring depends on:
1. Partner's carrier probability = population carrier frequency (2q)
2. Probability both pass on pathogenic allele = 1/4 (each carrier has 1/2 chance)
3. Combined: 2q × 2q × 1/4 = carrier_freq × carrier_freq × 1/4

But the calculator simplifies: since one partner's carrier status is known, risk = carrier_freq_of_partner × 1/4 ≈ carrier_frequency / 4.

### Affected Individual (risk = carrier_frequency / 2)

Applies to: homozygous, compound het confirmed, compound het assumed.
Scenario: The index patient is affected. One parent is an obligate carrier.
1. Partner's carrier probability = population carrier frequency (2q)
2. Probability affected parent passes on pathogenic allele = 1/2 (not 1/4, because we know they have it)
3. Combined: 2q × 1/2 = carrier_frequency / 2

::: warning Important Distinction
The risk divisor changes based on patient status: /4 for heterozygous carriers, /2 for affected individuals. This reflects whether the index patient's pathogenic allele status is known (carrier) or confirmed from both alleles (affected).
:::

## Assumptions and Limitations

Bullet list:
- Hardy-Weinberg assumes random mating and no selection — holds well for rare recessive conditions
- gnomAD is a population reference, not a diagnostic database — some populations are underrepresented
- The approximation 2pq ≈ 2q introduces < 0.1% error for allele frequencies under 5%
- Allele frequency aggregation assumes independent pathogenic variants (no linkage disequilibrium)
- The calculator does not account for reduced penetrance or variable expressivity

# Variant Filters

1-2 sentence intro: "The calculator automatically selects pathogenic variants from gnomAD using a set of configurable filters. This page explains each filter, its default setting, and how it affects your results."

## How Filtering Works

Brief overview: When you search for a gene, the calculator queries gnomAD for all variants in that gene. Filters determine which variants are included in the carrier frequency calculation. You can adjust filters globally in settings or override them per calculation.

## Filter Defaults

Default filter settings table:

| Filter | Default | Description |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| Loss-of-function (LoF HC) | Enabled | LOFTEE high-confidence loss-of-function variants |
| Missense | Enabled | Missense, inframe insertion, inframe deletion |
| ClinVar Pathogenic/Likely Pathogenic | Enabled | ClinVar P/LP classifications |
| ClinVar star threshold | 2 stars | Minimum review confidence |
| Include conflicting | Disabled | Variants with mixed classifications |
| Conflicting threshold | 80% | Required P/LP percentage for conflicting variants |

## Loss-of-Function (LoF HC)

Explain:
- Includes variants annotated by LOFTEE as high-confidence loss-of-function on the canonical transcript
- Types: stop gained, frameshift, splice donor/acceptor variants
- **Key point: LoF HC is independent of ClinVar.** A variant can be included based solely on its LOFTEE annotation, regardless of whether it has a ClinVar classification. This reflects the principle that high-confidence LoF variants are likely pathogenic by mechanism.
- LOFTEE (Loss-Of-Function Transcript Effect Estimator) filters out low-confidence LoF variants that may be rescued by downstream events

## ClinVar Pathogenic/Likely Pathogenic

Explain:
- Includes variants classified as Pathogenic (P) or Likely Pathogenic (LP) in ClinVar
- The star threshold controls the minimum review confidence (default: 2 stars)
- Star levels: 0 = no assertion criteria, 1 = assertion criteria provided, 2 = criteria provided + multiple submitters with no conflicts, 3 = reviewed by expert panel, 4 = practice guideline
- Higher star thresholds = more conservative (fewer variants included, higher confidence)

## Missense Variants

Explain:
- Includes missense, inframe insertion, and inframe deletion variants
- **Key point: Missense variants ALWAYS require ClinVar P/LP evidence.** Unlike LoF HC variants, a missense variant cannot be included based on predicted effect alone — it must have ClinVar Pathogenic or Likely Pathogenic classification that meets the star threshold.
- This prevents unsupported missense inclusion, which would inflate carrier frequency estimates

::: warning LoF HC vs. Missense: Different Evidence Requirements
LoF HC variants are included based on LOFTEE annotation alone (mechanism-based evidence). Missense variants require ClinVar P/LP classification (clinical evidence). This distinction reflects the higher confidence in loss-of-function predictions compared to missense predictions.
:::

## Conflicting Classifications

Explain:
- Disabled by default. When enabled, variants with "conflicting classifications" in ClinVar can be included if a sufficient percentage of submissions classify them as P/LP.
- The conflicting threshold (default: 80%) sets the required percentage
- Example: A variant with 5 P/LP submissions and 1 VUS submission = 83% P/LP, which meets the 80% threshold
- Use with caution — conflicting classifications indicate genuine uncertainty about pathogenicity

## Per-Calculation Override

Explain:
- In the variant table (Step 3), you can manually include or exclude individual variants regardless of filter settings
- This is useful for variants with disputed pathogenicity (like CFTR c.1210-11T>G)
- Manual overrides apply to the current calculation only — they do not change your global filter settings

## Template System Overview

Brief: Templates are structured text documents with `{{variable}}` placeholders that get replaced with your calculation results. The calculator includes pre-built templates for German and English in three clinical perspectives. You can customize templates through the settings dialog.

## Variable Reference

Complete table of all 14 template variables:

| Variable | Category | Example Value | Description |
|----------|----------|---------------|-------------|
| `{{gene}}` | Gene | CFTR | Gene symbol |
| `{{carrierFrequency}}` | Frequency | 4.0% | Carrier frequency as percentage |
| `{{carrierFrequencyRatio}}` | Frequency | 1:25 | Carrier frequency as ratio |
| `{{recurrenceRiskPercent}}` | Risk | 0.25% | Recurrence risk as percentage |
| `{{recurrenceRiskRatio}}` | Risk | 1:400 | Recurrence risk as ratio |
| `{{source}}` | Context | gnomAD v4.1.0 | Data source attribution |
| `{{indexStatus}}` | Context | heterozygous | Index patient status |
| `{{statusIntro}}` | Context | A homozygous pathogenic... | Status-specific intro sentence |
| `{{populationName}}` | Context | European (non-Finnish) | Population name (optional, empty if global) |
| `{{pmid}}` | Context | 12345678 | PubMed ID (optional, for literature source) |
| `{{accessDate}}` | Context | January 19, 2026 | Formatted data access date |
| `{{genderSuffix}}` | Formatting | *innen | German gender-inclusive suffix |
| `{{patientNominative}}` | Formatting | der Patient | German nominative form |
| `{{patientGenitive}}` | Formatting | des Patienten | German genitive form |
| `{{patientDative}}` | Formatting | dem Patienten | German dative form |

::: tip Using Variables in Custom Templates
Variables use double curly braces: `{{variableName}}`. If a variable has no value (e.g., `{{pmid}}` when no PubMed ID is provided), the placeholder is replaced with an empty string. Design your template text to read naturally even when optional variables are empty.
:::

## Perspectives

Explain each of the 3 perspectives:

### Affected Patient (`affected`)
For a patient with confirmed disease. The text describes the inheritance pattern, notes that parents are likely carriers, and presents recurrence risk for offspring.

### Healthy Carrier (`carrier`)
For an identified carrier who is not affected. The text explains what carrier status means, recommends partner testing, and presents the combined risk if the partner is also a carrier.

### Family Member (`familyMember`)
For a relative who may want cascade testing. The text describes the familial variant, the risk of being a carrier based on degree of relatedness, and the option for targeted genetic testing.

## Template Sections

All templates are divided into 8 sections that can be individually enabled or disabled:

| Section | Purpose | Typical Content |
|---------|---------|-----------------|
| `geneIntro` | Gene and disease introduction | Gene name, associated condition, basic disease description |
| `inheritance` | Inheritance pattern | Autosomal recessive inheritance explanation |
| `carrierFrequency` | Carrier frequency data | Population carrier frequency with source |
| `recurrenceRisk` | Risk calculation | Recurrence risk based on patient status |
| `populationContext` | Population specifics | Population-specific frequency if applicable |
| `founderEffect` | Founder effect note | Highlighted if population frequency > 5x global |
| `sourceCitation` | Data source | gnomAD version, access date, optional PMID |
| `recommendation` | Clinical recommendation | Testing recommendations for partner/family |

Table of 4 gender-inclusive styles:
