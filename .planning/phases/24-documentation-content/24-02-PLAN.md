**The 7 net-new questions (from RESEARCH.md):**

Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium category (3 questions):
1. **"When does Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium not hold?"**
   Answer (~4 sentences): Cover the five HWE assumptions (random mating, no selection, no migration, no new mutations, large population). Explain founder effects, assortative mating, inbreeding as clinically relevant violations. Note that for rare autosomal recessive conditions, HWE holds well enough because selection against carriers is minimal.

2. **"Why do carrier frequency estimates differ between gnomAD versions?"**
   Answer (~4 sentences): Explain dataset expansion (v4.1 has more samples than v2.1.1). Different populations are represented differently across versions. Exome vs genome composition varies. Reference genome differences (GRCh37 vs GRCh38) can affect variant calling.

3. **"What does it mean if gnomAD flags a variant as deviating from Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium?"**
   Answer (~4 sentences): gnomAD runs HWE tests on each variant site. Significant deviation may indicate genotyping artifacts (systematic sequencing errors) rather than true biological deviation. gnomAD filters some variants for HWE deviation. The calculator uses gnomAD's quality filters, so most HWE-deviant artifacts are already excluded.

gnomAD Data Interpretation category (4 questions):
4. **"What is the difference between exome and genome data in gnomAD?"**
   Answer (~4 sentences): Exomes sequence only protein-coding regions (~1.5% of genome) and provide the largest sample sizes. Genomes sequence everything and capture intronic and regulatory variants. gnomAD v4.1 combines both. The calculator aggregates allele counts from exomes and genomes where available.

5. **"How does gnomAD define genetic ancestry groups?"**
   Answer (~4 sentences): gnomAD uses principal component analysis (PCA) on genetic data to infer ancestry, not self-reported ethnicity. Groups include African/African-American (AFR), Admixed American (AMR), Ashkenazi Jewish (ASJ), East Asian (EAS), European (Finnish) (FIN), Middle Eastern (MID), Non-Finnish European (NFE), South Asian (SAS). These reflect genetic similarity clusters, not social categories. Population-specific carrier frequencies are most accurate when ancestry is well-represented in gnomAD.

6. **"What is allele number (AN) and why does it matter for carrier frequency estimates?"**
   Answer (~4 sentences): AN is the total number of alleles sequenced at a given site (roughly 2x the number of individuals). Low AN means few individuals were sequenced at that position, making the allele frequency estimate unreliable. Variants with very low AN should be interpreted with caution. The calculator shows AN alongside allele counts for transparency.

7. **"Why might a gene show no qualifying variants in the calculator?"**
   Answer (~4 sentences): Possible reasons: the gene has no ClinVar pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all pathogenic variants are structural or in regions poorly covered by gnomAD exomes, the gene is not well-represented in the selected gnomAD version, or variant filtering criteria (LoF HC, ClinVar threshold) exclude all variants. Try adjusting filters or checking a different gnomAD version.
