- title: "What is Carrier Frequency?"
- description: SEO-optimized meta description (~155 chars) about carrier frequency in genetics
- head: Article JSON-LD using the three-element tuple pattern `[script, {type: application/ld+json}, |content]`
  - @type: Article
  - headline: same as title
  - description: same as meta description
  - datePublished: "2026-02-23"

  - author: { @type: Person, name: "Bernt Popp", url: "https://github.com/berntpopp" }

Content structure (follow RESEARCH.md outline):
1. **Introduction** (~150 words): Carrier concept with analogy. Introduce autosomal recessive. Briefly mention dominant and X-linked exist for context, then narrow to recessive.
2. **Dominant vs Recessive Inheritance** (~200 words): Comparison table (dominant: one bad copy = affected; recessive: two bad copies needed). Explain why "carrier frequency" only matters for recessive.
3. **Why Carrier Frequency Matters** (~300 words): 1-in-4 risk per pregnancy for two carrier parents. CFTR example: ~1:25 European carriers. Inline CTA: `[Try it with CFTR ->](https://gnomad-carrier-frequency.kidney-genetics.org/?gene=CFTR)`.
4. **How Hardy-Weinberg Connects to Carrier Frequency** (~300 words): Show 2pq formula once. Explain intuitively that knowing allele frequency (q) predicts carrier frequency without counting every carrier. Brief worked example with numbers.
5. **Clinical Context** (~300 words): Cascade testing, reproductive counseling, population-based screening programs. Research Use Only framing.
6. **End-of-page CTA** (~50 words): Section directing to calculator with prominent link.

**File 2: `docs/concepts/how-to-calculate.md` (~1,200 words)**

Frontmatter must include:
- title: "How to Calculate Carrier Frequency"
- description: SEO-optimized meta description (~155 chars) about calculating carrier frequency from gnomAD
- head: Article JSON-LD (same pattern as above, different headline/description)

Content structure (follow RESEARCH.md outline):
1. **Introduction** (~100 words): Goal is understanding the method, not deriving it. Show HWE formula, explain variables. Inline CTA to calculator.
2. **Step 1: Identify Disease Allele Frequency** (~200 words): gnomAD provides AC (allele count) and AN (allele number). Formula: q = AC/AN. For multiple variants, sum allele frequencies. CFTR example.
3. **Step 2: Apply Hardy-Weinberg** (~150 words): Carrier frequency = 2pq, approximated as 2q for rare variants. Explain why approximation works (p close to 1). Numeric example continuing from Step 1.
4. **Common Conditions: Carrier Frequencies at a Glance** (~250 words): Comparison table with 7 genes. Use these genes ONLY (all verified for short-read gnomAD data): CFTR, GJB2, HEXA, HFE, PAH, ASPA, PKHD1. Table columns: Condition | Gene | Approximate Carrier Frequency | Population Note | Try in Calculator (deep-link). Present values as approximate from gnomAD v4.1 with caveat that actual values vary by ancestry. Each row links to `https://gnomad-carrier-frequency.kidney-genetics.org/?gene=GENENAME`. Add a note below the table: "Values are approximate global estimates. Carrier frequencies vary significantly between genetic ancestry groups. Use the calculator for population-specific data."
5. **Population-Specific Variation** (~200 words): Why global differs from population-specific. Founder effect concept. CFTR in Ashkenazi Jewish. HEXA in Ashkenazi Jewish. Link to calculator's population breakdown.
6. **Limitations to Know** (~200 words): gnomAD covers only sequenced variants; some ancestries underrepresented; HWE assumptions. Link to methodology page: `[Methodology](/reference/methodology)`. Research Use Only framing.
7. **End-of-page CTA** (~100 words): Strong section with gene-specific deep links.

**Both pages must include:**

- Approachable-expert tone: professional but explains jargon on first use, uses analogies occasionally
- No citations or references section -- content is self-contained
- No Punnett squares or custom diagrams
- Tables for visual elements only

- English-only content (no German)

**Carrier frequency values for the comparison table** (approximate, present with caveat):
- CFTR: ~1 in 35 (global), higher in European/Ashkenazi
- GJB2: ~1 in 30 (global), varies widely
- HEXA: ~1 in 300 (global), ~1 in 30 Ashkenazi Jewish
- HFE: ~1 in 9 (European, C282Y), much lower globally
- PAH: ~1 in 50 (global)
- ASPA: ~1 in 300 (global), ~1 in 40 Ashkenazi Jewish
- PKHD1: ~1 in 70 (global)

Note: These are approximations from literature. The table should direct users to verify via the calculator. Do NOT claim these are exact gnomAD v4.1 values. Frame as "approximate estimates from population studies."
